# International Case Studies & Design Patterns: How Ride-Hailing, Taxi & Mobility Apps Create Non-Ride, High-Frequency Engagement (and How AI Is Used), 2022–2026

## 1. Super-app and ecosystem strategies (Grab, GoTo/Gojek, Careem, Yandex Go, DiDi, Kakao T, Japan's GO and S.RIDE, Bolt): what share of users use multiple services, and does cross-service engagement raise ride frequency?

### Takeaway
Super-apps now report that a large share of active users use two or more services: about two-thirds of Grab's monthly transacting users (MTUs) and 40% of Uber's consumers (both Q4 2025). They say these users are more loyal and spend more, but none publishes a clean figure like "multi-service users ride X% more." The published frequency evidence covers single products or user cohorts instead: Grab Saver has 1.5x average frequency, and Group Order doubles retention and frequency.

Mobility-first apps without a food-delivery business add more car and travel services instead:
- **Kakao T (Korea):** parking, valet, designated drivers, EV charging, trains and flights.
- **GO (Japan):** premium dispatch, AI reservations, corporate accounts and gift tickets.

These two are the closest analogs for yoxi and the Hotai/Toyota ecosystem.

### Inferences
- **Benchmark for "daily."** Even Grab, the leading SEA super-app, has only about 17% of monthly transacting users transacting on a given day (7.8M of 47M). Uber users average about 6 rides plus deliveries a month (see Q2). Daily opens therefore have to come from non-transactional utility or content, not from daily transactions.
- **Where cross-service gains show up.** Published cross-service evidence mostly shows higher spend and retention per user, not big jumps in ride frequency. Uber's frequency rose only 2–4% a year in 2025–26 while membership grew 55% (Q2). The realistic goal for yoxi is to be the first app opened at car, travel and going-out moments, so that rides win share-of-wallet when a need appears.
- **Kakao T is the strongest structural analog for Hotai.** It turns car ownership into recurring app opens through parking, valet, designated drivers, EV charging and maintenance, and some of those can convert into rides (e.g., designated driver vs. taxi home after drinking).
- **GO shows the core service comes first.** The Japanese taxi app grew by being excellent at core dispatch (5-second assignment, 97% match rate) and adding premium, AI-reservation, corporate and gift layers rather than lifestyle content. Non-ride features cannot make up for unreliable dispatch.
- **Grab's "Everyday Guide" mirrors yoxi's framing.** Grab's 2026 positioning is nearly the same as yoxi's "城市移動與生活的夥伴" (a partner for city mobility and life). But Grab has published no adoption metrics for its AI concierge, so a hackathon team cannot cite proven outcomes for AI assistants in mobility. The proven numbers are for group, affordability and membership products.
- **DiDi's partner-brand membership is a template** for partner-funded reasons to open the app (checking perks), using Hotai's own partner network.

### Gaps
- **No clean frequency figure.** No company found publishes a 2022–2026 figure isolating "users of non-ride services take X% more rides." Grab and Uber give only aggregate multi-service shares and qualitative claims.
- **Undisclosed membership and user counts.**
  - GrabUnlimited subscriber count, Careem Plus member count and Kakao T MAU are not disclosed in the sources reviewed.
  - A third-party estimate of Kakao T MAU (AppGoblin, 779K) is implausibly low next to 30M sign-ups and should not be used.
- **Not verified, because the shared search budget ran out:**
  - DiDi's in-app AI assistant (reportedly "小滴")
  - Amap's 2025 "扫街榜," a restaurant ranking built from navigation data
  - Kakao Mobility's Fair Trade Commission (KFTC) fines over favoring its franchise taxis and blocking rival platforms, and its accounting dispute
  - GoTo's AI assistant and subscription products
  - Bolt rider-side metrics
  - S.RIDE user metrics
- **Method note:** a web-search cap shared across researchers was hit partway through. Later items were gathered by fetching known primary pages directly, so China and Korea coverage is thinner than Uber, Grab and Lyft.

## 2. Uber: Uber One, Eats cross-use, Going Out/Dine Out, Uber Travel, commute and price-lock products, advertising (Journey Ads), and AI features (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
Uber is the best-documented case. Uber One had 50M members in Q1 2026, and members drive about half of gross bookings. In 2025–26 Uber launched a steady stream of reasons to open the app before a ride is needed:
- AI commute alerts
- price-lock and prepaid ride passes
- Dine Out with OpenTable, including ride discounts after a reservation
- itinerary-aware travel features
- hotels via Expedia
- AI voice bookings

Uber's own KPIs show monthly trips per user are flat to slowly rising (6.1–6.2 per monthly active platform consumer, or MAPC; +2–4% YoY), so growth mostly comes from more users. Its loyalty history also includes shutting down a free points program (Uber Rewards, 2022) and an FTC dark-pattern lawsuit over Uber One (2025).

## 4. MaaS and journey-planning/navigation apps (Whim/MaaS Global, Toyota "my route", Jelbi, Moovit, Citymapper, Transit, Waze, Google Maps): what makes them habit-forming daily, and what failed?

### Takeaway
The mobility apps people open daily without booking anything are navigation and transit utilities (Google Maps, Waze, Transit, Moovit, Citymapper). They answer "when should I leave, is my bus coming, what's happening on my road," and they build habit through real-time, personal, often crowdsourced information plus light community gamification.

Pure B2C MaaS subscriptions largely failed commercially. MaaS Global (Whim) went bankrupt in March 2024 after raising €149M from investors including Toyota, with only about 10,000 MAU in Helsinki and a 2022 loss of €9.3M on €3.8M revenue. City-backed MaaS (Jelbi) and utility-first planners lasted.

### Cited Findings

#### Whim / MaaS Global (Finland): failure
- **The numbers:**
  - Filed for bankruptcy in March 2024, after announcing the Whim shutdown.
  - Raised €149M from investors including BP Ventures, Toyota and Mitsubishi.
  - About 10,000 active monthly users in Helsinki.
  - 2022 losses of €9.3M on €3.8M turnover; staff of 38 in 2022, down from a peak of 120 in 2020.
  - MaaS Alliance: the B2C subscription model was "not commercially sustainable at present," while B2G and B2B models are more viable.
  - Source: [Traffic Technology Today, Mar 21, 2024](https://www.traffictechnologytoday.com/news/mobility-as-a-service/maas-global-declares-bankruptcy.html)
- **Diagnosis:**
  - Urban Sharing's Tom Nutley: users "want the flexibility to pick and choose the mode, pay in real-time rather than be tied into a large subscription." A subscription may suit only a primary mode.
  - Whim had operated in Vienna, Antwerp, Helsinki, Turku, Tokyo, Birmingham and across Switzerland.
  - Source: [Zag Daily](https://zagdaily.com/trends/maas-global-is-dead-long-live-maas/)
- **Wider MaaS record:**
  - Whim provided about 1.8M trips a year after launch.
  - Gothenburg's UbiGo subscription trial was well received (some private cars were deregistered during it) but was discontinued for lack of government support for third-party resale of transit tickets.
  - Transport for West Midlands' MaaS "did not live up to expectations."
  - Deutsche Bahn's Qixxit was sold in 2019.
  - Source: [Wikipedia: Mobility as a service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobility_as_a_service)

#### Jelbi (Berlin): city-backed MaaS that persisted
- **What it offers:**
  - One app for Berlin's public transport and sharing services (bus, train, e-moped, e-scooter, bike, car, taxi, van). Users register once and pay for everything.
  - Options can be compared "by time, price, occasion, weather, or mood."
  - Physical Jelbi stations at S-Bahn and U-Bahn stations.
  - "Jelbi points for micromobility" at Berlin Central Station.
  - Source: [Jelbi](https://www.jelbi.de/en/home/)
- Trafi's CEO cites Jelbi, Brussels' Floya and the UK's Breeze as evidence MaaS works in city-backed form. — [Zag Daily](https://zagdaily.com/trends/maas-global-is-dead-long-live-maas/)

#### Toyota-related: my route (Japan)
- The current site pitches my route as "「行きたい」をつなげる「おで活」アプリ," an outing app for collecting places you want to visit (an "おでかけメモ" outing memo), plus route search and discount tickets. The page does not name the operator. — [my route](https://top.myroute.fun/)

#### Transit app: crowdsourcing plus gamification
- **GO crowdsourcing:**
  - Riders who start GO share their vehicle's real-time location, shown to others as an avatar. This fills gaps where agencies lack real-time data.
  - Rate-My-Ride micro-surveys ask about crowding, timeliness and accessibility.
  - Monthly and all-time **leaderboards per line** show "how close you are to the #1 spot" on your most-used line, with a progress bar and a monthly recap.

  - GO uses about 5% battery per 20-minute trip and less than 100 KB of data.
  - Source: [Transit Help Center](https://help.transitapp.com/article/91-what-is-go-crowdsourcing)
- Transit covers 1,130+ metro areas in 34 countries. Its 2018 $17.5M round was led by Alliance Ventures (Renault-Nissan-Mitsubishi). — [Wikipedia: Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transit_%28app%29)

#### Moovit
- **Moovit:**
  - 800M users (May 2020), when Intel acquired it for $900M.
  - A volunteer "community editor" program since 2014; users report delays and crowding.
  - Revenue from B2G analytics and a location-based advertising platform (July 2022).
  - Source: [Wikipedia: Moovit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moovit)

#### Citymapper
- **Citymapper:**
  - Monetization attempts included a night-bus service (Sept 2017 – July 2019, discontinued) and the Citymapper Pass subscription (February 2019).
  - 2019 revenue was £5.8M against net losses above £9M, followed by retail crowdfunding (May 2021).
  - 50M+ users in 100 cities (2023); acquired by Via in March 2023.
  - Source: [Wikipedia: Citymapper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citymapper)

#### Waze
- **Community model:** users report crashes, jams, hazards and police; volunteer map editors gain rank from their edits; points reward reporting. — [Wikipedia: Waze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waze). Waze also runs communities of map editors, beta testers and localizers ("Where drivers help drivers"). — [Waze company page](https://www.waze.com/company)
- **AI features:**
  - Conversational Reporting launched in 2025. In July 2026 it was expanded so drivers can suggest map edits in everyday language, interpreted by Gemini.

  - Motorcycle mode that learns a driver's habits.
  - Source: [Stuff, Jul 13, 2026](https://stuff.co.za/2026/07/13/waze-adds-ai-powered-features-that-seem-surprisingly-useful/)

#### Google Maps
- **November 2025 Gemini features:**
  - **Gemini in navigation:** hands-free multi-step requests (e.g., find a budget vegan restaurant on the route, check parking, navigate there), asking about popular dishes, adding Calendar events, and voice incident reports.

- **Local Guides:** points for reviews, photos and place-fact edits, and for adding places or roads. Levels unlock benefits such as early access to features. — [Google Local Guides](https://maps.google.com/localguides/)
- **Privacy:** Timeline (location history) moved to on-device storage, with optional end-to-end-encrypted cloud backup, per-place deletion of activity, and blue-dot controls showing location status. — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products/maps/updates-to-location-history-and-new-controls-coming-soon-to-maps/)

### Inferences
- **The daily habit loop in mobility apps:**
  - Trigger: an upcoming departure or a disruption.
  - Action: a glance.
  - Reward: certainty about ETA, crowding and price.
  - Investment: reports and ratings that improve the service for everyone.

  This maps onto the Hook model in Q5. Ride-hailing apps rarely own the "when to leave" moment; Uber's Commute Alerts and Grab's "plan when to leave" in GrabMaps are explicit attempts to take it from Maps.
- **Being the daily planner is not the same as being profitable.** Whim and Citymapper show this. Money comes from transactions, partners (B2B/B2G) or ads, and subscriptions only work for a primary mode.
- **A direct lesson for the Toyota group.** Toyota invested in MaaS Global, and the most fragile model was the bundled B2C subscription. A utility layer (when to leave, parking, airport, events) with pay-as-you-go ride conversion is lower-risk.
- **Crowdsourced contribution can be a durable non-ride open.** Waze, Transit and Moovit are examples, but only where contributions visibly improve everyone's real-time experience. In a taxi context, possible equivalents are pickup-point notes, accessible-entrance tips and queue reports at stations or airports.

### Gaps
- **Missing user metrics:** current MAU/DAU for Waze and Google Maps; Transit user counts and Transit Royale subscription economics; Moovit users after 2020; Jelbi station and user counts.
- **Unfetched study:** the Whimpact behavioral study (Ramboll) PDF could not be fetched.
- **Toyota my route:** operator, areas and metrics could not be verified; the site page shows only positioning.
- **Proactive alerts:** no published data on how proactive traffic alerts change Google Maps open frequency.

## 5. Design patterns with evidence: proactive/commute notifications, loyalty points and tiers, streaks and gamification (durable vs. novelty), content feeds and local discovery, eco rewards, social/community, AI companions; and habit-formation research

### Takeaway
1. **Proactive prompts work, but the effects are small and fatigue is real.** A micro-randomized trial found a 3.9% lift in engagement within 24 hours. Vendor surveys report that 10% of users disable notifications after just one push a week.
2. **Gamification fades.** It produces a novelty spike that declines after about 4 weeks and only partly recovers, and then only when the underlying utility is real.
3. **Streaks work when tied to a genuine recurring behavior and built with slack** (Duolingo: +1.7% D7 retention from a streak animation).
4. **Group and social mechanics have the strongest disclosed retention effects in mobility super-apps** (Grab Group Order: 2x retention and frequency).
5. **For inherently infrequent products, DAU/MAU is the wrong target.** The aim is to own high-intent moments and monetize them.

### Cited Findings

#### Retention and habit-formation benchmarks
- **Retention curves (Quettra data, 125M Android devices, 2015):**

  | | D1 | D3 | D30 | D90 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Average app | 29.17% | 23.42% | 9.55% | 3.97% |
  | Top-10 apps | 74.67% | — | 59.80% | 50.87% |

  - "The key to success is to get the users hooked during that critical first 3-7 day period."
  - Spammy "We Miss You" messages are unlikely to help.
  - Source: [Andrew Chen](https://andrewchen.com/new-data-shows-why-losing-80-of-your-mobile-users-is-normal-and-that-the-best-apps-do-much-better/)
- **Power User Curve (L30 activity histogram):** DAU/MAU is not the right metric for products not designed for daily use. Instead, ask whether revenue can be earned during the moments users are engaged. — [Andrew Chen](https://andrewchen.com/power-user-curve/)
- **"Law of Shitty Clickthroughs":**
  - Every engagement tactic decays: a 1994 HotWired banner had a 78% click-through rate, vs. 0.05% for Facebook banners in 2011.
  - Mobile notifications are expected to decay as novelty fades.
  - Useful information sustains response.
  - Source: [Andrew Chen](https://andrewchen.com/the-law-of-shitty-clickthroughs/)
- **Hook model:** trigger → action → variable reward → investment. Habits form when internal triggers (routines, emotions) replace external ones such as notifications or icons. — [Nir Eyal](https://www.nirandfar.com/how-to-manufacture-desire/)
- **Time to habit (Lally et al., UCL, 2009):** automaticity took 66 days on average. Missing one opportunity did not significantly matter, but very inconsistent people did not form habits. Performing the behavior in a consistent context is key. — [UCL News](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2009/aug/how-long-does-it-take-form-habit)

#### Push notifications and proactive prompts
- **Micro-randomized trial (JOOL well-being app, 1,255 users, 89 days, 2017):**
  - A tailored push made users 3.9% more likely to engage within 24 hours (risk ratio 1.039).
  - The effect was largest at 12:30 pm (8.8%) and on weekends.
  - Habituation over 12 weeks was not statistically significant.
  - The authors used availability rules (e.g., at most one prompt a day) to avoid users disabling notifications.
  - Source: [Bidargaddi et al., JMIR mHealth 2018](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2018/11/e10123/)
- **Opt-in and fatigue (vendor studies, mostly 2015–2021):**
  - Median push opt-in: 81% on Android vs. 51% on iOS (Airship, H1 2021).
  - Reaction rates: 4.6% on Android, 3.4% on iOS.
  - 6% of users abandon an app after one push a week (Upland).
  - 10% disable notifications after one weekly push, and 40% at 3–6 pushes (Helplama).
  - 62% consider too many pushes to be spam (VWO).
  - Source: [Business of Apps compilation](https://www.businessofapps.com/marketplace/push-notifications/research/push-notifications-statistics/) (vendor data; dated)
- **Mobility examples of utility pushes:**
  - Uber's AI Commute Alerts: best time to request, based on ride history, driver supply and price trends. — [Uber newsroom, Go-Get 2025](https://www.uber.com/newsroom/go-get-2025/)
  - Google Maps proactive traffic alerts even when not navigating. — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products/maps/gemini-navigation-features-landmark-lens/)
  - Grab's flight, check-in and gate reminders. — [Grab GrabX 2026](https://www.grab.com/sg/press/others/grab-unveils-13-ai-powered-experiences-at-grabx-2026-as-southeast-asias-intelligent-everyday-guide/). Grab's flight-tracked airport bookings. — [Vulcan Post](https://vulcanpost.com/884812/grab-new-ai-features-what-to-expect/)
  - Uber's flight-tracked Reserve airport pickups. — [Uber AU newsroom](https://www.uber.com/au/en/newsroom/travelfeatures/)

#### Gamification, streaks and the novelty effect
- **Rodrigues et al. (2022), 14-week quasi-experiment with 756 students:**
  - The gamification effect began to decline after about 4 weeks (a novelty effect).
  - It partly recovered in weeks 6–10 (a familiarization effect) without any design change, but did not return to the initial effect size.
  - Studies shorter than about 12 weeks may mislead.
  - Source: [Rodrigues et al., Int. J. Educ. Technol. High. Educ., 2022](https://educationaltechnologyjournal.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s41239-021-00314-6)
- **Duolingo (Jan 2022):**
  - A new animation when a streak is extended raised D7 retention for new learners by 1.7%.
  - Allowing two Streak Freezes raised daily active learners by 0.38%, without encouraging more days off.
  - Learners with a 7-day streak are 3.6x more likely to finish their course (correlational).
  - Design lessons: make early progress feel big, rely on loss aversion later, and add slack.
  - Source: [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit/)
- **Gamification in mobility apps:**
  - Transit: per-line leaderboards and monthly recaps. — [Transit Help Center](https://help.transitapp.com/article/91-what-is-go-crowdsourcing)
  - Waze: points and editor ranks. — [Wikipedia: Waze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waze)
  - Google: Local Guides levels. — [Local Guides](https://maps.google.com/localguides/)
  - Grab: monthly "Power Up" challenge and coins for reviews. — [Grab rewards](https://www.grab.com/sg/rewards/)

### Inferences
- **Utility first, gamification on top.** Putting the evidence together, the most durable non-ride opens are utility-led: departure timing, disruption alerts, price certainty, travel logistics, parking and EV status. Gamification can be a light layer on top. Points or streaks without an underlying recurring need should be expected to fade after about 4 weeks (Rodrigues), and a hackathon demo should not claim durable engagement from gamification alone.
- **A good streak follows a real behavior.** A commuting streak or a weekly "city outing" streak fits this, with Duolingo-style slack (freeze days for holidays and work-from-home days). Rewards should be in ride credits so the loop closes back to transactions.
- **Notification design follows from the trial and fatigue data:**
  - one well-timed, high-utility push beats many
  - mid-day and weekend timing tends to work better
  - availability rules prevent fatigue
  - the trigger should be the user's own schedule or an external event (rain, disruption, flight delay), not a marketing calendar
- **Proactive AI must be easy to ignore.** It should be opt-in and clearly useful; Snapchat's My AI is the counterexample. It is safer to let an AI suggestion appear inside an existing utility moment (e.g., the commute card) than as a persistent new chat surface.
- **Group and family features have the best published retention evidence** in mobility super-apps (Grab Group Order 2x). They also suit Taiwanese use cases: family accounts for elderly parents, shared commutes, group outings.

### Gaps
- **Proactive mobility notifications:** no peer-reviewed or company study found, within budget, measuring their effect on ride-hailing open frequency or conversion. There is no public uptake data for Uber Commute Alerts or Google's proactive alerts.
- **Eco gamification:** no evidence found that it produced lasting mode shift in ride-hailing contexts. Ant Forest has no published causal data on behavior change.
- **Academic MaaS research:** papers on MaaS adoption and gamification in transport were not retrieved, because of the search cap.

## 6. Monetization and conversion: how non-ride engagement has been turned into ride demand or partner/advertising revenue, with published uplift numbers

### Takeaway
Published conversion evidence clusters around five mechanisms:
1. **Commitment passes:** Lyft Price Lock holders took about 4 more rides a month.
2. **Partner-loyalty linking:** more than 25% of Lyft's North American rides are partnership-linked, and linking reactivated lapsed riders.
3. **Capturing intent before a trip:** itinerary import → reserved airport rides; restaurant reservation → discounted ride; merchant-funded event offers.
4. **Paid membership that shifts share of wallet:** Uber One members account for about 50% of gross bookings.
5. **Advertising on in-app and in-ride surfaces:** Uber's ad business reached a $1.5B run-rate (May 2025), and Lyft exited 2025 above $100M.

Almost no company publishes conversion rates from discovery or lifestyle surfaces (Dine Out, Travel) into rides.

### Inferences
- **Closing the loop for yoxi needs two parts.** First, a ride-redeemable currency earned through non-ride actions that partners pay for (restaurants, hotels, parking, car service, events), as with Uber One credits and GrabCoins. Second, intent-capture flows that place a ride button, with a discount, at the moment of planning (reservation, itinerary, event ticket, flight).
- **For the hackathon pitch, the conversion metrics with external precedent are:**
  - extra rides per pass holder (Lyft: +4 rides/month)
  - share of rides linked to a partnership (Lyft: 25%+)
  - members' share of bookings (Uber: ~50%)
  - retention and frequency of group products (Grab: 2x)
  - ad revenue per ride or a run-rate figure (Uber, Lyft)

- **Ads should monetize engagement, not create it.** Ads worked for Uber and Lyft because in-ride time already exists. They are a revenue complement, not a reason to open the app.

## 7. Failures and cautionary tales: features or apps that tried to raise engagement but failed, shut down, annoyed users, or triggered privacy backlash

### Takeaway
The recurring failure modes are:
- **Non-core lifestyle verticals with weak unit economics.** Gojek's GoLife services were shut down in 2019–2020, and Careem quit ride-hailing in Pakistan in 2025 to refocus.
- **Bundled B2C MaaS subscriptions.** Whim went bankrupt in 2024.
- **Mobility services with low density.** Citymapper's night bus and Waze Carpool both closed.
- **Free points programs.** Uber Rewards was shut in 2022.
- **Retention dark patterns.** The FTC sued Uber over Uber One in 2025, citing up to 23 screens and 32 actions to cancel.
- **AI forced into the interface.** Snapchat's My AI drew review-bombing in 2023.
- **Misuse of location and behavior data.** DiDi was fined ¥8.026B in 2022 and pulled from app stores for 18 months. Uber settled with the FTC over privacy in 2017. Strava's heatmap revealed military bases in 2018.
- **Novelty decay and notification fatigue.**

#### Privacy and location-data backlash
- **DiDi (China):**
  - On July 4, 2021, the Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC) ordered DiDi removed from app stores, "citing violations around the company's collection and usage of personal information" (25 apps in total).
  - In July 2022 DiDi was fined ¥8.026B (about US$1.2B).
  - It was barred from registering new users until January 2023.
  - Source: [Wikipedia: DiDi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DiDi)
- **Uber FTC settlement (Aug 15, 2017):**
  - The FTC alleged that Uber's claim of closely monitoring employee access to rider and driver data was false: monitoring was largely abandoned within a year.
  - Sensitive data, including geolocation, sat as plain text in cloud backups.
  - Settlement: a comprehensive privacy program with biennial third-party audits for 20 years.
  - Source: [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2017/08/uber-settles-ftc-allegations-it-made-deceptive-privacy-data-security-claims)
- **Strava:**
  - The Global Heatmap (published Nov 2017) was found in Jan 2018 to reveal military bases (in Syria, Afghanistan, and the UK nuclear submarine base HMNB Clyde).
  - Later incidents: segment flaws exposing Israeli base personnel (2022), home addresses revealed by heatmaps (2023), and movements of world leaders' security teams (2024–25).
  - Strava responded by letting users opt out of heatmap aggregation and simplifying privacy settings.
  - Source: [Wikipedia: Strava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strava)
- **Waze:**
  - Police agencies objected to police-location reporting (2014–15).
  - In 2016, UC Santa Barbara researchers showed a Sybil attack (fake devices) could track users.
  - In 2019, flaws in the Live Map let users be tracked.
  - Source: [Wikipedia: Waze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waze)
- **Google's privacy-first redesign of location history:** Timeline stored on the device, encrypted backups, per-place deletion. This sets a design benchmark for using frequent-places data. — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products/maps/updates-to-location-history-and-new-controls-coming-soon-to-maps/)

### Inferences
- **Stay adjacent to the core trip.** Lifestyle services far from it (laundry, massage, beauty) failed even inside Gojek. The Gojek, Careem and Whim stories point the same way: non-ride engagement features should sit next to the trip (before, during or after), not in unrelated verticals.
- **Personal location and behavior data is central to the yoxi brief, and the backlash triggers are known:**
  - non-consensual collection or enrollment
  - features that are hard to turn off or remove
  - aggregated maps that can reveal individuals
  - opaque employee access

  Design responses: process frequent places on the device where possible (Google Timeline), use an explicit opt-in "my places / my routines" set-up, offer a "ghost mode" (Transit), make deletion easy, never share by default, and say plainly why each suggestion appeared.
- **Easy cancellation protects memberships.** Transparent, easy-cancel memberships avoid the regulatory and trust damage Uber faced, and honest savings figures should net out the fee.
- **AI companions should be summoned, not pinned.** The Snapchat episode shows the cost of forcing a new AI surface on users.
