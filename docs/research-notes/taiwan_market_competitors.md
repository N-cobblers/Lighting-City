## 1. Market structure: fleet size, market size, players and estimated shares

### Takeaway
Taiwan has about 92,300 registered taxis (May 2026). The total has barely moved in six years, but the mix is shifting quickly toward app-only 多元化計程車: about 11.7% in 2020, about 30% in H1 2025, and "6:4" according to one industry estimate. App dispatch is concentrated in a few players:
- 台灣大車隊 55688: about 29,000 vehicles; claims 46–47.5% of the dispatch market.
- Uber: undisclosed volume, #2 by vehicles; has owned 皇冠大車隊 since February 2026.
- LINE GO: the former LINE TAXI, now controlled by Yulon; 15,000+ drivers; calls itself #3.
- yoxi: 10,000+ drivers.
- Bolt: entered in September 2025.
- Legacy fleets, such as 大都會 55178.

No independent share study exists, and drivers commonly work several apps at once.

### Cited Findings

- Registered taxis: about 92,303 at end-May 2026. Nationwide there are about 100,000 taxi plates (約10萬張). About 90% of plates are in the six special municipalities, mostly held by 車行, and nearly 6,000 plates are vacant — [公視 2026-06-22](https://news.pts.org.tw/article/814182)

### Inferences
- **Two different "shares" are in circulation.** 55688's ~29,000 vehicles are about 31% of the 92,303 registered taxis (my calculation), which matches the "~30% of taxis" figure. The 46% and 47.5% figures are framed as shares of the *dispatch* market, and the methodology is undisclosed. Safest wording: "about 30% of all taxis; claims ~46–47.5% of dispatch."
- **Driver counts cannot be added up into market shares.** 55688 (~29k) + LINE GO (~15k) + yoxi (~10k) + Uber (~60k in 2023) far exceeds the ~65,000 platform-affiliated drivers, so many drivers run several apps. For example, yoxi drivers can take Uber Taxi orders (section 2). Passengers are the opposite: 80% use only one app (section 3). **Passenger loyalty, not driver lock-in, is where the competition happens.**
- **Probable ranking by dispatched volume:** 55688 > Uber > LINE GO ≈ yoxi > Bolt and legacy fleets. This is inferred from fleet sizes and company statements; no independent data exist.
- **Rough national volume (my estimate, with stated assumptions):**
  - Method A: 92,303 taxis × 13.0 trips/day (the Taipei full-time average) × 300–365 days ≈ **360–440M trips/year**. This is an upper bound, because not every registered taxi runs daily at Taipei utilization.
  - Method B: 55688 claimed ~80M trips/year in 2022 with ~24% of taxis, which implies about 330M nationally if utilization were equal. The real total is probably lower, because dispatch-fleet cars are likely busier than average.
  - Combined order of magnitude: **~250–440M taxi trips/year.**
  - App-booked share: sources range from ~40% (Uber's "60% street hail") to 75% (55688's "online"), which gives roughly **100–330M app-booked trips/year**.
  - Per capita, with Taiwan's ~23M population, that is roughly **11–19 taxi trips per person per year on average**, and heavy users account for much of it.
- The fleet total has been flat at about 92k since 2020, so platform growth must come from taking share, the shift to 多元, and services adjacent to rides. This is why every competitor is diversifying (section 9).

## 2. Regulation that shapes the market (公路法, 多元化計程車, Uber's model, changes 2023–2026)

### Takeaway
The rules limit how platforms can compete on price and supply:
- Yellow-cab meter fares are government-approved.
- 多元 cars are app-only, cannot be hailed or queue at stands, and must show the fare upfront.
- Each vehicle may affiliate with only one fleet, and cross-platform dispatch is not allowed.
- Motorcycle passenger service is illegal.

Uber operates through licensed taxi fleets and has owned one since 2026. The main 2025–2026 changes are:
- A trial opening airports to 多元 pickups (effective 2026-05-17; Kaohsiung first).
- Reform of 車行 fees.
- A Bolt-led push to allow multi-fleet affiliation.
- Pending fare increases.
- Competition-law decisions shaping Uber's food-delivery position.

### Cited Findings

**Birth of 多元化計程車 (2016–2017)**
- Kaohsiung launched 多元 on 2016-12-29. Rules: smartphone-app booking only; cars need not be yellow (sedans, SUVs and vans allowed); fares up to 2× the regular fare; vehicles at most 5 years old; drivers need the same certification as taxi drivers. The government framed the scheme as a response to Uber — [公視 2016-12-23](https://news.pts.org.tw/article/344651)
- Taipei launched in March 2017. Operators complained that app-only booking and the rule that fares must sit above regular taxi rates hurt competitiveness — [公視 2017-08-31](https://news.pts.org.tw/article/369489)

**Uber's path to the taxi-fleet model (2019–2020)**
- The draft amendment to 汽車運輸業管理規則 §103-1 (the "Uber條款") required rental-car-with-driver hires to exceed one hour and banned queuing and cruising for passengers. About 12,000 contracted drivers were affected — [INSIDE 2019-04-25](https://www.inside.com.tw/article/16210-uber-103-1-protest). The page metadata shows 2025-12-02, which conflicts with the visible 2019 byline.
- MOTC planned to fine rental–Uber cooperation from October 2019 and encouraged Uber drivers to switch to 多元 — [TechNews 2019-09-05 (search snippet)](https://technews.tw/2019/09/05/uber-driver-worried-about-unemployment-because-of-new-amendment-rule/)
- MOTC relaxations in 2019: looser rules on number of doors; taxis may skip the meter when the app announces the fare in advance; plates can transfer across districts; legal entities may operate taxis — [監察院 109交調007](https://cybsbox.cy.gov.tw/CYBSBoxSSL/edoc/download/41668)
- By February 2020, 3,798 converted Uber-linked rental cars made up 38.2% of all 多元 — [監察院](https://cybsbox.cy.gov.tw/CYBSBoxSSL/edoc/download/41662)
- Uber "does not run its own fleet"; drivers must join one of its partner fleets, about 16 in February 2024 — [數位時代 2024-02-16](https://www.bnext.com.tw/article/78389/yoxi-uuber). Uber reported 24 fleet partners in 2023 — [Uber newsroom](https://www.uber.com/zh-TW/newsroom/uber10). It has owned 皇冠大車隊 since 2026-02-05 — [工商](https://www.ctee.com.tw/news/20260205700953-431401)
- Uber Taxi (優步小黃) charges the same meter fare as street-hail taxis — [工商 2025-06-25](https://www.ctee.com.tw/news/20250625702132-431401)

**Rules on fleet affiliation and cross-dispatch**
- 「計程車客運服務業申請核准經營辦法」 §16 allows a vehicle to be entrusted to only one taxi service business ("one driver, one fleet"). Bolt recruiting drivers already registered elsewhere could put those drivers in a gray area — [數位時代 2025-09-24](https://www.bnext.com.tw/article/84554/2025-uber-yoxi-55688-bolt)
- The yoxi–Uber deal (2024-02-16) is driver-side only: yoxi drivers can take Uber's 優步小黃 orders. Passengers cannot cross-book between the two apps, because government rules do not allow cross-dispatch between platforms — [數位時代 2024-02-16](https://www.bnext.com.tw/article/78389/yoxi-uuber)
- A Bolt-commissioned survey (台灣趨勢研究, 500 platform drivers, March–April 2026) found:
  - 89.6% want to work with several platforms or fleets.
  - 90% call the single-fleet rule unfair.
  - 87.4% support changing the law.
  - Sources: [工商 2026-05-26](https://www.ctee.com.tw/news/20260526701975-430503); [聯合 2026-05-26](https://udn.com/news/story/7266/9527314). The sponsor has a commercial interest in the result.

**多元 operating rules and fares**
- 多元 cars may not be hailed on the street or queue at stands (不可路招、不可排班) — [鏡週刊 2025-11-12](https://tw.news.yahoo.com/%E5%8F%AB%E8%BB%8Aapp%E5%A4%A7%E6%88%B02-bolt%E7%99%BB%E5%8F%B0%E5%8D%A1%E4%BD%8D%E7%82%BA%E5%93%AA%E6%A8%81-%E7%AB%9F%E8%88%87uber%E7%9B%A4%E7%AE%97%E6%9C%89%E5%BF%97-%E5%90%8C-222858610.html)
- 多元 fares may vary (surge multipliers, peak and off-peak, special requests), but the app must show the estimated route and fare at booking. Under the 公路法, yellow-cab fares are drafted by associations and unions and approved by the authority. Overcharging carries a fine of NT$9,000–90,000 — [工商 2026-01-09](https://www.ctee.com.tw/news/20260109700926-431401). The fine is set by 公路法 §77 — [工商 2026-01-19](https://www.ctee.com.tw/news/20260119701495-431401)

### Inferences
- Fares are regulated, drivers are locked to one fleet, cross-dispatch is banned and new modes like motorcycles are blocked. Platforms therefore compete on **experience, reliability, service tiers, loyalty and adjacent services**, not on price or supply. This is the space a yoxi between-ride engagement play would occupy.
- Passengers face no lock-in, which is why competitors spend on loyalty programs and promotions.
- The airport opening gives 多元 platforms high-value trips that are booked in advance. Those trips come with natural pre-trip touchpoints (flight status, luggage, timing) that could justify opening the app before a trip.
- Any feature that drifts toward unlicensed transport, such as carpool or 順風車, or motorcycles, carries real regulatory risk. The response to Uber's Beitou trial shows how enforcement-minded regulators are.

### Gaps
- Not verified: whether the Kaohsiung airport 多元 trial actually started in June 2026, and which platforms joined.
- Pending as of 2026-10-09: the outcome of the Taipei fare proposal and the FTC decision on Grab–foodpanda (due 2026-10-27).
- The current statutory text of the 多元 rules (fare caps, vehicle-age limits) was not pulled from the official law database.

## 3. Usage frequency, app analytics and retention evidence

### Takeaway
There is no public DAU/MAU, open-frequency or retention data for Taiwan ride-hailing apps. What evidence exists shows a low-frequency, need-driven category:
- Taxis are too small a share of trips to appear as their own mode in Taipei's mode-share statistics.
- 80% of ride-hailing users rely on a single app.
- Demand clusters around evenings, weekends, nightlife, stations and airports.

Proxies for reach: Uber has about 590k iOS ratings and ranks #8 in Travel; 55688 has about 140k ratings and ranks #9. User bases: 55688 has 7.2M app users, LINE GO 5.17M members, and yoxi over 1M active members.

### Cited Findings
- **Mode share.**
  - National public-transport share was 14.3% in 2022, a record low; motorcycles were 45.8% — [TVBS 2024-07-21](https://news.tvbs.com.tw/life/2558146), citing MOTC's 民眾日常使用運具狀況調查.
  - Taipei residents' weekday trips in 2024: walking 20.8%, private car 20.1%, motorcycle 16.3%, MRT 16.0%; green modes 63.0%. **Taxis are not reported as a separate category** — [台北市主計處 2025-07-16](https://www-ws.gov.taipei/001/Upload/367/relfile/46908/9442607/0bbf97f1-faca-4f2c-b296-3013a137e3f0.pdf)
- **How riders get a taxi (sources conflict):**
  - A 全國計程車產業提升推動協會 survey: over 55% of riders most often use an app; street hail and phone booking about 20% each — [數位時代 2025-09-24](https://www.bnext.com.tw/article/84554/2025-uber-yoxi-55688-bolt)

- **Subscription engagement in a Taiwan mobility comparator.** WeMo PASS members (NT$99/month) are about 20% of users but ride 4× as much as regular users and generate over 40% of revenue — [TechNews 2026-02-14](https://technews.tw/?p=1514086)

### Inferences
- **Ride-hailing is opened when there is a need, not by habit.** The average Taiwanese takes roughly 11–19 taxi trips a year (section 1 estimate), the median far fewer, and usage is concentrated in a heavy-user tail (for example, the 2,000-trip Tainan user). With 80% of users on a single app, the incumbents' real fight is to be the **default app at the moment of need**. Daily opens are not their core metric. This confirms yoxi's problem statement and suggests the challenge is creating new reasons to open the app, rather than out-promoting rivals.
- iOS ratings counts roughly suggest Uber's installed base is several times 55688's. But 55688 has more members overall, many of them probably phone users, which fits 55688's older, business and phone-dispatch customer base.
- yoxi's users skew toward deal-seeking (Z.com). Points and rewards may drive engagement but tend to attract users who leave when incentives stop. 55688 makes the same point when it calls subsidies "假性需求" (section 5).
- WeMo PASS shows that in Taiwan mobility a subscription can concentrate frequency, since a minority of subscribers drives over 40% of revenue. This is analogous to Uber One (section 4).

### Gaps
- **No public DAU, MAU, open-frequency, session or retention data** from Sensor Tower, data.ai or Similarweb for any Taiwan ride-hailing app was found. Only a 2022 Sensor Tower download ranking, cited in company PR, surfaced.
- No national survey on how often individual Taiwanese take taxis or use ride-hailing was found. The MOTC 計程車營運狀況調查 and 運具 survey microdata were not accessible.
- The LINE GO and yoxi App Store pages failed to load, so their current chart ranks are unknown. Google Play data were not checked.

## 4. Uber Taiwan: engagement and loyalty tactics

### Takeaway
Uber's between-ride engagement comes from Uber Eats, linked through the **Uber One** subscription. In July 2026 Uber raised Uber One prices by about 66% (NT$120 → 199 a month), replaced the flat 5% ride discount with points of up to 15%, and added family sharing and overseas use. Around this it adds family and teen accounts, scheduled and airport rides, a courier service, bank tie-ins, a Wrapped-style year-in-review, referrals and celebrity brand campaigns. No rider-facing generative-AI feature was found in Taiwan, and member counts are not disclosed.

### Cited Findings
**Uber One (subscription)**
- **Before July 2026:**
  - Launched in Taiwan in 2022; NT$120/month or NT$1,200/year.
  - Benefits: $0 Uber Eats delivery on orders of NT$199+, 5% off rides.
  - Uber said annual members saved an average of over NT$3,800 in H1 2024, more than 3× the fee.
  - Cancellation flow offered retention deals: 90% off the next month for monthly members, about 40% off for annual members.
  - A September 2024 social-media "unsubscribe wave" claimed members paid more than non-members. Uber denied it, and the outlet's own test showed members did save.
  - Source: [數位時代 2024-09-25](https://www.bnext.com.tw/article/80637/uber-eats-fee-expensive)

  - Prices: monthly NT$120 → **199**; annual NT$1,200 → **1,990**; student monthly NT$84 → 139; student annual NT$840 → 1,393. New members pay immediately; existing members from their first renewal after **2026-07-13**. 工商 lists the monthly increase as +59.16%, but 120 → 199 is actually +65.8%.
  - **Ride points up to 15%**, previously up to 5%: Premier 15%; Standard and several others 8%; Uber Taxi 3%; new 8% tier for 優快送.
  - **Family sharing**: one family member shares benefits for free.
  - **"Uber One優惠隨行"**: benefits work in other Uber One countries.

**Strategic moves**
- Bought 皇冠大車隊 (closed 2026-02-05) and plans to join government social-care transport programs for the elderly and pregnant women — [工商 2026-02-05](https://www.ctee.com.tw/news/20260205700953-431401)
- The foodpanda deal was blocked, then abandoned (section 2). Uber agreed to buy Delivery Hero in July 2026 — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/business-and-practice/uber-agrees-to-buy-delivery-hero-as-food-sector-consolidates)

### Inferences
- **Uber is the only Taiwan ride-hailing player with a high-frequency sister service (food delivery) under one account and one subscription.** That is the structural reason Uber can generate opens between rides. yoxi cannot copy it directly.
- Moving from a flat 5% discount to points (up to 15%) that can only be spent inside Uber turns savings into a **stored balance that pulls users back** to rides or Eats.
- The ~66% price rise suggests Uber judges heavy users locked in, or needs to monetize as delivery costs rise (Uber denies the link). Either way, Taiwan's market leader treats subscription as its main frequency tool.
- Teen and family accounts create recurring, parent-facing touchpoints such as school-commute notifications. This is a way into daily routines that 55688 and LINE GO, in the sources reviewed, do not emphasize.
- Uber's engagement is transactional. No Taiwan-local lifestyle content, transit integration or proactive daily information features were found.

### Gaps
- Uber One member numbers, Uber Eats' standalone Taiwan share, Uber app open frequency, Uber for Business Taiwan details, and any planned Taiwan AI-assistant features were not found.

## 5. 台灣大車隊 55688: engagement and loyalty tactics

### Takeaway
55688 states the most explicit "beyond rides" strategy in Taiwan. It calls itself a 「生活服務媒合平台」 and a 「生活服務超級App」, treats rides as the "high-frequency, low-margin" traffic source, and tries to sell "low-frequency, high-margin" life services:
- 1,000+ life-service categories, with about 120–127k providers.
- Designated drivers, airport transfers, instant delivery, laundry, cleaning, auto care and an online mall.
- In-car media.

Its loyalty tools are mostly about payment:
- Fares can be paid with third-party points (Happy Go, LINE Points, JKOPAY, Asia Miles and others).
- Five-tier 熊友會 membership.
- 永豐55688 co-branded card.

AI is used mainly for dispatch, in partnership with Google. Results: revenue grew 8% in H1 2026; instant-delivery and airport orders are rising. Management admits its biggest challenge is the perception that 55688 is "only a ride-hailing app."

### Cited Findings
**Strategy**
- Positioning and goals:
  - Described as 「生活服務媒合平台」 spanning mobility, lifestyle, logistics and services.
  - Ride-hailing is "high-frequency, low-margin" and life services "low-frequency, high-margin"; the goal is to **use ride traffic to drive life-service use**.
  - Target: users meet daily needs "within 3 to 6 minutes".
  - About 127,000 life-service providers.
  - Its first driver-commission change in 20 years (2025), which it says is still the industry's lowest.
  - 20th-anniversary rebrand with spokesperson 楊祐寧 and the slogan 「一直都在，無處不載」.
  - The chairman says the next 20 years will focus on AI and data sovereignty.
  - Source: [風傳媒 2025-12-09](https://www.storm.mg/article/11086708)
- Super-app build-out:
  - Work started in 2022. Life services grew from 5 to over 1,000 categories by integrating the PRO360 expert network.
  - 2025 additions: 找專家, 機場接送 (in-app booking up to 30 days ahead, with 2× compensation if service fails) and 即時快遞.
  - CEO 林念臻: "the biggest challenge is changing the public's perception of 55688 as only a ride-hailing app."
  - 2025 target: over 20% growth.
  - Source: [數位時代 2025-01-07](https://www.bnext.com.tw/article/81927/2024-55688)

### Inferences
- 55688 *reverses* yoxi's framing. It treats rides as its frequency engine and life services as the upsell. No daily-use feature (content, information, check-ins, streaks) was found.
- Its points strategy brings outside currencies in: users can pay fares with points they earned elsewhere. This lowers friction and can make a ride feel free, but it does not give people a 55688-specific reason to come back between rides. 熊友會 tiers reward ride volume, not opens.
- Non-ride revenue looks large (peripheral sales plus life matching were ~58% of 9M-2025 revenue). However, "平台周邊銷售" probably includes driver-side vehicle, parts and financing sales, so it should not be read as consumer cross-sell. This is an inference; the segment's contents are not itemized.
- Monetizing in-ride screen attention shows the ride itself is a high-attention moment, and one a companion app could use as well.

### Gaps
- 熊友會 tier thresholds and member distribution, app MAU and open frequency, life-service order volumes and repeat rates, and AI dispatch performance metrics were not disclosed.

- **Being present every day has not produced ride growth.** Core trips rose only about 3% while planned and social use cases (airport, short rentals, enterprise, groups) grew quickly. Visibility alone does not create trips; value tied to the user's plans and social life does. This is a useful lesson for an "AI 出行夥伴".
- The "Social MaaS" direction (family groups, booking for others, shareable passes, carpooling, the 聊不夠 slogan) bets that **social context** (meeting friends, family logistics) brings people back more often than individual commuting.
- **LINE GO is yoxi's closest rival on an AI travel companion.** It is already testing MCP-connected agentic trip planning. A hackathon concept should assume LINE GO could offer conversational trip planning inside LINE, and should differentiate on proactive, everyday value or on Hotai-ecosystem data rather than on trip planning alone.

## 8. Daily-use city and mobility apps that already win Taiwanese users' daily opens

### Takeaway
The apps Taiwanese open daily or weekly win through:
- a recurring routine need (commute, parking, payment);
- stored value or points that work like money;
- required steps such as card binding or insurance enrollment;
- subscriptions that make each extra use cheaper.

Perks and lotteries are the weakest lever; 台北捷運Go members criticized them. Ride-hailing apps lack the daily-routine need, which is the core of yoxi's problem.

- **E-payment acceptance on ride apps**: 55688 and 大都會 accept many e-wallets (悠遊付, 全支付, 街口 and others); LINE GO centers on LINE Pay; yoxi and Uber accept fewer — [money101 2026-06-10](https://www.money101.com.tw/blog/%E5%8F%B0%E7%81%A3%E5%A4%A7%E8%BB%8A%E9%9A%8A-55688-%E4%BF%A1%E7%94%A8%E5%8D%A1-%E5%84%AA%E6%83%A0)
- **Parking** (the name 停車大聲公 is verified):
  - **停車大聲公** (by 宏碁智通): parking search with remaining spaces, fee estimates and comparison, **automatic street-parking payment** (for example in Taichung), a **subscription plan 「優惠久久」** and 停車金 credits.
  - Others named: 車麻吉 (payment plus navigation), uTagGO (points, auto-deduction), ViVi Park (street-parking payment), USPACE (space booking).
  - Source: [roo.cash 2026-03-21](https://roo.cash/blog/?p=111882). The article gives conflicting coverage figures: "10,000+ lots" vs "700+".

### Inferences
- Taiwanese daily-use mobility apps build habit through: (1) a routine trigger every day (commute, parking, payment); (2) money-like stored value, such as Metro points convertible to 悠遊付 or e-wallet points; (3) required steps (YouBike insurance, card binding); (4) subscriptions that cut marginal cost (WeMo PASS, parking plans).
- Perks and lotteries alone underperform: 台北捷運Go members asked for fare credit instead.
- **A ride-hailing app usually comes *after* these apps in the user's decision** (check transit or the map, then decide to call a car). The moments right before that decision (rain, a missed last train, an HSR arrival, a late night out, parking unavailable) are where a ride-hailing app could earn opens without a booked ride. **This is inference; no competitor was observed doing this systematically.**
- yoxi's sister services (iRent, WeMo via the iRent alliance, Hotai owners' cars) overlap with several of these daily routines. Whether Hotai already integrates parking or e-payment is outside this scope.

### Gaps
- Not found (search budget exhausted): user counts for 悠遊付, iPASS MONEY, 全支付 and 街口; Google Maps usage in Taiwan; bus apps (Bus+, 台北等公車); 台鐵 e訂通; T Express app usage share; national YouBike ridership and app membership.

## 9. Trends 2024–2026

### Takeaway
The market is consolidating and diversifying. Six trends stand out:
- Uber bought a fleet and Yulon took LINE TAXI; Grab is entering via foodpanda.
- Bolt brought commission and price competition.
- 多元 is heading toward parity with yellow cabs, and airports are opening to it.
- Fares and driver costs are rising while the driver pool ages.
- The EV transition is slow (about 2% of taxis in 2024, against a 50% target for 2030).
- Every platform uses AI for dispatch, only LINE GO is testing agentic AI, and platforms are expanding into delivery, designated drivers, rentals, scooters and enterprise.

Loyalty is being repriced: Uber One is up 66% and LINE GO has moved to 5 tiers.

### Inferences
- Growth is coming from the shift to 多元, airport and long-distance trips, and adjacent services, while core in-city ride volume is mature (LINE GO +3%). For yoxi, **engagement features that lead into planned, high-value trips** (airport, HSR, events, group outings) are more likely to show measurable results than generic daily content.
- Uber raised subscription prices just as competition intensified (Bolt, plus its own fleet), which suggests it is confident its heavy users are locked in.
- All the main players are converging on the same playbook: MaaS/super-app breadth, points, tiers and AI dispatch. **Proactive, non-trip, everyday value delivered by an AI companion is still largely unclaimed in Taiwan** among the sources reviewed (this is an absence of evidence, not proof). LINE GO's agentic experiments mean that gap may not stay open long.

## 10. Cross-competitor comparison: between-ride engagement levers and evidence they work

### Takeaway
Taiwan competitors use five kinds of levers:
1. Subscription or points currency: Uber One; LINE GO points and tiers; 55688 熊友會 and external points.
2. Super-app breadth: 55688 life services; LINE GO's 7 services.
3. Building into a high-frequency host: LINE GO in LINE; Uber with Eats; yoxi with Hotai.
4. Gamified or social campaigns: LINE GO.
5. In-ride media: 55688 TOLiFE; LINE GO Growth screens.

Evidence that these levers raise *between-ride opens* is almost nonexistent publicly. The evidence that does exist concerns trips and revenue, and it points to limited ride-frequency gains: LINE GO trips +3%, and 55688's admitted perception problem.

- **No Taiwan ride-hailing competitor reports app-open frequency, DAU/MAU or engagement uplift from its loyalty tactics.** Every public result metric is trips, revenue or members. A yoxi hackathon pitch that proposes and measures *open frequency without a ride* would be novel in this market's public discourse.
- Each lever links to an existing habit: payment (points), eating (Uber Eats), chatting (LINE) or saving money (coupons). The open opportunity is to link yoxi to **daily movement decisions**: weather, transit disruption, events, parking, car maintenance, airport and HSR timing. These recur daily and, in the sources reviewed, no competitor serves them proactively.
- Points that can only be spent inside one ecosystem (Uber One, 和泰Points) keep users within it. Points from outside currencies (55688) make paying easier but do not build loyalty to 55688. A yoxi design probably benefits from **和泰Points as the earned currency for non-ride engagement** (for example, check-ins or useful actions), which matches Uber's 2026 move to points.

### Gaps
- No third-party measurement of loyalty-program effectiveness, churn or app-open frequency for any Taiwan ride-hailing app was found.
- Participation and uplift data for LINE GO's gamified campaigns and 55688's life-service cross-sell were not disclosed.
